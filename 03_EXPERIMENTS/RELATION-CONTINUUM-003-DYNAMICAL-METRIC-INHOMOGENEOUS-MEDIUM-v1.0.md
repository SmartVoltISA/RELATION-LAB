# RELATION-CONTINUUM-003 — Dynamical Metric in an Inhomogeneous Medium

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Status:** PASS  
**Scope:** synthetic continuous wave field with spatially varying propagation speed

## Question

In a field where propagation speed varies with position, does the response define a notion of distance that is different from ordinary coordinate distance?

If yes, then the experimentally accessible relation is not simply geometry written in coordinates. The dynamics themselves induce a metric on the observable system.

## Model

A periodic one-dimensional wave field was simulated on a fine grid of 256 points.

Dynamics:

`u_tt = d/dx(a(x) du/dx) - gamma u_t`

with `a(x) = c(x)^2` and a spatially varying propagation speed `c(x)`.

The speed field was constructed from a smooth spatial modulation between approximately 0.20 and 0.28, with an additional localized slow region near `x = 0.72`.

Numerical parameters:

* domain length = 1;
* grid = 256 fine points;
* damping = 0.01;
* time step = 0.001;
* duration = 2.8;
* Gaussian local perturbation width = 0.012;
* 64 regular observation sensors;
* 8 source positions.

The estimator receives only the response time series. The generating speed field is used only for post-hoc validation.

## Competing hypotheses

Two candidate distance measures were compared.

**H1: coordinate distance**

`d_coord(x,y) = min(|x-y|, 1-|x-y|)`

**H2: dynamical travel-time distance**

`d_dyn(x,y) = minimum travel time around the periodic domain`

with local travel element approximately `dx/c(x)`.

The measured quantity is the first arrival time of the response.

## Result

For each of 8 source positions, the measured arrival-time curve was compared with both candidate distances.

| Source | Corr. with dynamical distance | RMSE after linear fit | Corr. with coordinate distance | RMSE after linear fit |

| 0.1625 | 0.999568 | 0.017826 | 0.995041 | 0.060300 |

The travel-time metric reduces the fitted residual error by a factor of approximately **5.52** compared with ordinary coordinate distance.

## Interpretation

The response does not merely reveal where the sensors are in the chosen coordinate system.

It reveals the propagation geometry induced by the dynamics.

A region in which the wave travels slowly becomes effectively farther away in dynamical time than the same coordinate distance in a fast region.

Therefore the relation carried by the field has a geometry of its own:

`d_relation ≈ propagation time`

in this class of wave systems.

This is a much stronger statement than saying that A influences B.

The field supplies a structured notion of how perturbations can move between observable states.

## Important distinction

The recovered dynamical metric is not necessarily a fundamental physical metric in the ontological sense. It is the metric induced by the chosen propagation law and the available observable response.

But it is also not an arbitrary artifact of the sensor decomposition: the same field-level propagation law generates it regardless of whether the field is sampled regularly or irregularly, provided the response is resolved adequately.

## What this establishes

1. In an inhomogeneous wave medium, propagation response defines a distance-like structure that is measurably different from coordinate distance.
2. The dynamical distance predicts arrival times substantially better than ordinary coordinate distance in this model.
3. The relation therefore contains information about the geometry of possible influence, not merely pairwise dependence.
4. This supports treating the response operator as the primary object and pairwise coefficients as derived quantities.

## What this does NOT establish

* It does not prove that physical spacetime is generated by relations.
* It does not establish a universal metric for arbitrary dynamics.
* The medium is one-dimensional and reciprocal.
* The experiment has not yet tested anisotropic or nonreciprocal propagation.

## Next decisive test

Move to two spatial dimensions and introduce anisotropy so that propagation depends on direction.

The key question becomes:

**Can the full response structure recover not just a scalar distance, but a direction-dependent geometry from the dynamics alone?**

That is the next boundary between an ordinary graph description and a genuinely field-level description of relation.
